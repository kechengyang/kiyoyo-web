# POP MART UI pattern study

Research source: [POP MART US](https://www.popmart.com/us), reviewed on 2026-08-05 at desktop and 390 px mobile widths. The site is dynamic, so observations come from the rendered home page and product-detail experience rather than static HTML extraction.

## What creates the visual character

POP MART's interface is restrained; the campaign and product imagery create most of the personality.

- Use a thin announcement strip above a quiet, white header.
- Center the brand mark and balance it with search and utility actions.
- Keep category navigation compact, uppercase, widely spaced, and secondary to campaigns.
- Use a full-width campaign carousel with one campaign per slide, short copy, restrained controls, and one or two clear actions.
- Give sections generous vertical whitespace and simple centered headings.
- Present releases as horizontal discovery rows with date or launch context.
- Make product imagery dominant. Keep cards flat, borderless or barely bordered, and free of long descriptions.
- Keep PDPs sparse: media gallery on the left; title, price, fulfilment, options, primary actions, and accordions on the right.
- On mobile, preserve campaign impact while shifting product collections to horizontal scrolling and compact controls.
- Use black and white for most controls. Use brand red for logo, urgency, selection, promotion, and a small number of calls to action.

## Homepage anatomy observed

1. Announcement strip with a short benefit and optional detail link.
2. Header with search, centered logo, account, wishlist, and bag.
3. Secondary category navigation.
4. Large campaign carousel with side arrows and progress indicators.
5. Significant whitespace before a release or new-arrivals section.
6. Centered section title, optional launch-calendar link, and release timestamp.
7. Horizontal product row showing roughly four to five desktop cards.
8. Follow-on campaign or collection modules.
9. Community signup, support, social, and region controls in the footer.

## Product card anatomy observed

- Use a large, consistent image area as the card's main element.
- Keep the background neutral and the card itself flat.
- Show a concise name and price under the image.
- Add only one or two quiet utilities such as wishlist or release alert.
- Use a badge only when it changes a buying decision: new, launch, sale, low stock, or exclusive.
- Avoid ratings, long descriptions, multiple color chips, shadows, and stacked promotional stickers in browse views.

## Product detail anatomy observed

- Start with a quiet breadcrumb.
- Use a two-column desktop layout and a stacked mobile layout.
- Let the media gallery occupy more visual space than the purchase panel.
- Keep the buying sequence stable: status, title, price, promotion, fulfilment, specification, quantity, actions.
- Use strong black/white action contrast and red only for time-sensitive or promotional information.
- Put secondary details in accordions rather than a dense wall of text.
- Show related products after the primary purchase decision.

## Adopt, adapt, avoid

| Decision | POP MART pattern | KIYOYO application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adopt | Campaign art dominates the homepage | Use original KIYOYO fruit-tea, dessert, character, lifestyle, and collaboration art. |
| Adopt | Quiet white header and sparse navigation | Reduce large red header blocks; let the KIYOYO capsule logo be the concentrated brand signal. |
| Adopt | Flat, image-led product cards | Use for goods and curated drink discovery, with fewer borders, stickers, and shadows. |
| Adopt | Release-led horizontal rows | Use for seasonal drinks, limited drops, IP collaborations, and restocks. |
| Adopt | Sparse two-column PDP | Use for goods; adapt the right panel for drink customisation when the product is a drink. |
| Adapt | Character/category navigation | Map to Drinks, Desserts, Drops, Characters, Lifestyle, and Collaborations rather than copying POP MART categories. |
| Adapt | POP NOW/launch language | Use original names such as KIYOYO Drops, Fresh Drop, Drink Studio, or Today at KIYOYO. |
| Adapt | Fulfilment selector | Make selected Nairobi store, pickup/delivery eligibility, distance, and preparation time explicit. |
| Adapt | Mobile horizontal discovery | Keep swipe rows for campaigns and collections, but preserve a direct Drink Studio entry. |
| Avoid | Proprietary character art, logos, campaign layouts, and wording | Never reuse or closely redraw them. Generate or commission original KIYOYO assets. |
| Avoid | One-for-one page cloning | Recompose patterns around KIYOYO's five-tab product model and drink ordering requirements. |
| Avoid | Carousel everywhere | Use a carousel only when users benefit from sequential campaigns or a browsable collection. |

## KIYOYO interpretation

Use POP MART's confidence and restraint, not its identity. The UI should feel like a curated global lifestyle retailer; the content and core flow should feel local to KIYOYO: bright drinks, Nairobi stores, fast pickup, playful rewards, and collectible goods.
